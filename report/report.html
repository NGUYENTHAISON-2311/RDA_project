PRIDE PXD072052 · Sus scrofa domesticus · reanalysis

Notochordal EV Corona Atlas

Fourteen runs, seven preparations, paired vesicle and soluble fractions — remapped to human orthologs and read with the protein corona treated as biology rather than background.

01What this is

PXD072052 is the deposit behind the notochordal-cell extracellular-vesicle atlas, in which surface-adsorbed proteins — the corona — activate SMAD3/4 and suppress NFκB signalling in chondrocyte-like cells. If the corona does the signalling, it cannot be filtered out as contamination.

This reanalysis takes the deposited Proteome Discoverer protein groups, maps porcine accessions onto human gene symbols, keeps only proteins that reproduce across donors, and annotates them into vesicle, matrix, matricellular, growth-factor, surface and intracellular layers. Every enrichment test runs against the proteins this preparation actually contains, never the full human proteome.

The design makes one thing possible that most EV datasets cannot offer: each preparation was measured twice, once as vesicles (EV) and once as the soluble fraction left behind (SOL). That pairing turns “is this a co-isolate?” from an argument about annotation lists into a measurement.

02Three findings

The vesicle markers behave exactly as they should. Every canonical marker is enriched in the EV fraction — CD63, CD9, CD81, TSG101, the ESCRT components, flotillins, RAB27A/B. ALIX (PDCD6IP) and RAB27A appear in EV runs and in no soluble run at all. Nothing was assumed about which files were which; the assignment was tested against this biology before anything downstream ran.

The matrix is overwhelmingly soluble, but a defined subset rides the vesicles. Thrombospondins, tenascin-X, periostin, perlecan and versican sit almost entirely in the soluble fraction. Yet aggrecan, COMP, fibromodulin, CILP, collagen II, decorin, biglycan and SPARC are detected in all seven EV preparations. A negative EV/SOL ratio is not a verdict of contamination here — the soluble fraction is the secretome, so matrix proteins belong there; what marks a corona protein is reproducible presence on the vesicles regardless.

The EV-enriched corona is a annexin and S100 layer. The proteins most enriched on vesicles relative to solution are the calcium-dependent phospholipid binders — annexins A1, A2, A4, A5, A6, A7, A8 and S100A6/A10/A11 — alongside receptors (EGFR, IGF1R, INSR, ITGAV, CD36) and matrix regulators such as ADAM9. This is a coherent surface layer, not a scatter of leftovers.

03Getting the data out of a 223 GB archive

The processed results are deposited as a single ZIP64 archive of 223,172,625,425 bytes. It holds eighteen members, and almost all of that volume is Proteome Discoverer result databases of 56–77 GB each. The protein table is not one of them: it is 2.4 MB.

The PRIDE mirror advertises Accept-Ranges: bytes, so the pipeline reads the ZIP64 central directory from the tail of the file, locates the two members it needs by name, and inflates only those byte ranges — about 700 KB transferred in total. No raw files are downloaded and no spectra are re-searched.

The local copy was unusable. Eight of the ten previously downloaded .raw files were zero bytes and the archive fragment on disk was a truncated 19 MB that no ZIP reader would open. None of it was needed.

04Mapping pig to human, where most identifiers no longer exist

The study searched a December 2023 Sus scrofa database. UniProt has since pruned the porcine TrEMBL proteome heavily: of the 4,116 accessions are now deleted entries. A current-UniProt lookup alone silently loses more than half the atlas, and a quarter of the deposited rows carry no gene symbol to fall back on.

Identity is therefore resolved from four sources in turn — current UniProt, the UniParc archive, the deposited description, and the UniSave version history — then carried to human by NCBI orthology, symbol passthrough, Ensembl Compara, or matching of descriptive protein names against human SwissProt.

Fibronectin is genuinely absent. No fibronectin entry exists anywhere in the deposit — only fibronectin type-III domain proteins. This survived every mapping layer, so it is a property of the deposited results rather than a mapping failure, and is worth checking against the original search output.

05What survives donor reproducibility

A protein is retained when it is confidently identified in at least four of the seven EV preparations, matching the threshold used in the source publication. Match-between-runs quantification without an identification does not count toward detection.

06The corona axis

Each preparation contributes one paired EV–SOL comparison, so the contrast is a paired test across seven donors rather than a comparison of group means. Proteins absent from every soluble run are not imputed — absence is the measurement — and are tested on detection counts instead.

07Annotation layers

Layers are deliberately non-exclusive: a proteoglycan can also be a growth-factor reservoir. Each protein carries independent flags plus one primary class resolved by precedence, and two opinions are recorded side by side — what the protein is (curated lists and Gene Ontology) and where it actually partitions (the EV/SOL measurement).

Two definitions were deliberately narrowed. The GO term “extracellular exosome” is annotated to 978 proteins here, largely because it is populated by EV proteomics itself; using it to define the EV category would be circular, so the category uses curated MISEV markers and the GO term is kept only as a weak flag. Likewise “cytosol” propagates to most of the proteome, so the intracellular category uses specific organelles only.

08Enrichment against the detected EV proteome

The background for every test is the retained EV proteome itself — human symbols. Against the whole human proteome, “extracellular exosome” and “extracellular matrix” are enriched in any vesicle preparation, which tells you nothing. Against this background, a term is enriched only if it stands out among proteins the preparation already contains.

09The GAG machinery

Notochordal cells sit in a proteoglycan-rich nucleus pulposus, and glycosaminoglycan content is the functional readout of disc and cartilage repair. The atlas already reports the proteoglycan core proteins; this asks a different question — do the vesicles carry the enzymes that build the GAG chains onto them?

Six KEGG pathways define the machinery: chondroitin/dermatan sulfate, heparan sulfate, and keratan sulfate biosynthesis, GAG degradation, the UDP-sugar precursor supply, and the PAPS sulfate donor system. The last two are included because sulfation and nucleotide-sugar supply are mechanistically required, but their genes are flagged — several are general metabolic enzymes whose presence says nothing about GAG synthesis specifically.

The machinery is present and confidently identified. XYLT1 — xylosyltransferase-1, the committed first step of GAG chain initiation — carries 78 unique peptides and is detected in all seven EV preparations. PAPSS2 carries 63, UGP2 64, CSGALNACT1 30, CHST3 29. These are not marginal identifications.

But the direction is the finding, and it runs the other way.

Grouping by biosynthetic step separates two populations cleanly, and the split follows where each enzyme works in the cell — read off the measurement, not assumed from annotation. The Golgi-lumenal chain builders and sulfotransferases sit far to the soluble side. The cytosolic nucleotide-sugar and sulfate-donor enzymes sit close to zero and are retained almost completely: PAPSS1, PAPSS2, BPNT1, BPNT2, UGDH, UXS1, UGP2, GFPT1 are present in every EV preparation.

What this does and does not say. Retained means detected on the vesicles in at least four of seven preparations — the enzymes are really there. The negative ratios mean the same proteins are far more abundant in the soluble fraction, which for Golgi-resident type II membrane enzymes is consistent with shedding or with Golgi-derived material in the soluble pool. The EV-enriched compartment contains no chain-building or sulfating enzyme at all. So the pro-matrix effect of these vesicles is unlikely to work by delivering GAG-synthesis enzymes as cargo — a constraint on the mechanism, not a null result.

Enrichment against the detected EV proteome

These pathways hold ten to thirty-eight genes each. Tested through g:Profiler's gSCS correction against a 2,931-symbol background, none of them survives — which would report “nothing here” about pathways whose genes are demonstrably present and strongly directional. Absent entirely.

No hyaluronan synthase (HAS1, HAS2, HAS3) appears anywhere in the deposit, and neither does the N-deacetylase/N-sulfotransferase family (NDST1–4) that initiates heparan sulfate modification. These are listed as not-detected rows in the output table rather than omitted, because an absence you cannot see is indistinguishable from one you never tested.

10What to check before relying on this

  • Preparations are not confirmed as independent donors. P13–P24 are preparation identifiers from the deposit; whether each is a distinct animal or a pool is not recorded in the study metadata. Reproducibility here is across preparations.
  • The two batches are unbalanced — two preparations in TRY_0463, five in TRY_0500. Direction of the EV/SOL ratio agrees across batches for of the retained set; the rest should be treated cautiously.
  • 117 protein groups remain unmapped, mostly entries whose only description is a protein family ("Annexin", "Profilin", "RNA helicase"). They are carried through the tables as explicitly unmapped rather than guessed at.
  • The deposited table is one Proteome Discoverer export. The absence of fibronectin suggests confirming it against the original study output.
